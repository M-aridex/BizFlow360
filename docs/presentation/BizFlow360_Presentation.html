The Problem

Kenya's engine flies blind

  • MSMEs employ most of Kenya's workforce — yet fail silently.
  • No records, thin buffers, no early warning of distress.
  • Lenders can't assess risk → credit exclusion.
  • Existing tools serve big firms — not the duka, kibanda, shamba.

Gap  No accessible, explainable early-warning system for Kenyan MSMEs.

The duka, the kibanda, the shamba — Kenya's real economy.
Our Answer

Not just a verdict — a co-pilot

1 · PREDICT
Calibrated LightGBM distress score (0–100%) on real KNBS data.
2 · EXPLAIN
SHAP waterfall shows exactly why — fully transparent.
3 · ADVISE
Break-even math + NVIDIA AI → 10 tailored actions + PDF.

Wrapped in a WhatsApp-style chat, history tracking, and a mobile-friendly Streamlit app — as familiar as M-Pesa.

Method

One pipeline, data to advice

DataKNBS MSME survey · 15,815 records
→
Cleanplaceholders → NaN · impute · clip
→
Engineer23 features · ratios & flags
→
TrainLightGBM · 80/20 stratified
→
CalibratePlatt sigmoid · τ = 0.33
→
ShipSHAP + AI advice · Streamlit

Target: 0 = stable · 1 = distressed (KNBS self-reported business performance).

Scientific Rigour

We audited our own model — and fixed it

  • v1.0 cheated: profit features ≈ the answer key (target leakage) — AUC .70 but invalid.
  • v2.0 got honest: leakage-safe features + calibration → true probabilities (Brier .206).
  • v2.1 got useful: robust ratios + tuning + τ = 0.33 → recall 28% → 75%.

Principle  Honest first, then useful — never impressive-but-wrong.

A Product Decision

Why we moved the threshold to 0.33

28%
recall at 0.5
misses 7 of 10 distressed firms
75%
recall at 0.33
false positive = extra advice (cheap)
  • False negative = a distressed business told "you're fine" — the dangerous error.
  • We deliberately bias toward catching distress early.
